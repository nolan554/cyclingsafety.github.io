VISION ZERO

So what can be done in order to improve safety for cyclists in Boulder? We believe the future of cyclist safety in Boulder is dependent on a mixture of infrastructure, educational, and enforcement changes. Currently, the City of Boulder is focusing most of its energy and resources on infrastructure changes. The City of Boulder implemented its Vision Zero Action Plan in 2014 to reduce severe traffic accidents to zero. The focus of this policy has been to improve the most dangerous roads in Boulder. Cycling accidents are not evenly distributed in Boulder. In their 2022 Safe Street Report, the City of Boulder revealed that 67% of crashes happen on the City’s arterial streets, even though they are only 17% of the total street network.

The vision zero commitment is ending all traffic-related fatalities, hence the name “Vision Zero”. A quick look at their webpage makes it clear that their primary focus is on pedestrians and cyclists, as the first three photos prominently feature them on Boulder’s local streets. Vision Zero has identified hotspots recording crashes are recorded on a map to identify which streets are more problematic than others. With this information, they have a more specific point to start from and try to solve. Vision Zero thus far has implemented physical infrastructure designs, revised traffic signals, protected left turn phasing, and green pavement to indicate where the bike lane is. These are just a few examples of what Vision Zero does to make the streets safer and prevent crashes. Vision Zero also does a fantastic job of working with local authorities to put more enforcement on areas identified by the map to be more problematic. Targeted police enforcement helps prevent fatalities by speed checking cars and enforcing safer speed limits in hotspots. Boulder joined the initiative in 2014, and since then has been working toward the mission of “reduc[ing] the number of severe crashes (fatalities and serious injuries) to zero.” (Transportation Safety – Vision Zero | City of Boulder) Vision zero has guided many of Boulder’s infrastructure initiatives. The most important of which is the attentiveness and public nature of the data involved in their projects, emphasizing how projects are data-driven, increasing their likelihood of gaining community support. While Vision Zero has seen a lot of success in Boulder, deaths still occur. For example, earlier this year, Daniel Bench was killed at Edgewood and 24th street when a truck turning right hit him as he entered a crosswalk. He was 88. Vision Zero’s work is vital to preventing deaths like this. No matter who is at fault in these cases, the goal to reduce traffic deaths to zero remains the same.

INFRASTRUCTURE

Cycling deaths in Boulder remain a tragedy

Cycling deaths in Boulder remain a tragedy, but there is progress being made. The City of Boulder is already making significant improvements in the areas of infrastructure, education, and enforcement. We believe that these improvements have dramatically improved cycling safety in Boulder and support their development so that the number of cycling deaths in Boulder can eventually reach zero.

Folsom Street

The city has already undertaken several major infrastructure projects. This includes attempts to improve dangerous roads such as Folsom Street, Iris Avenue, and Baseline Road. In 2015, the City of Boulder first attempted to create a protected bike lane on Folsom St. The idea was to “right-size” the roads. This required reducing the number of car lanes to create space for other types of commuters. In this case, the project reduced the number of car lanes from four to three and added a protected bike lane. While this project was done in good faith, Westward claims it created a large hassle for both drivers and cyclists. “According to critics, it was definitely an example of good intentions gone awry by way of artificially created traffic jams that frayed the nerves of motorists and cyclists alike.” The immediate outrage from the Boulder community caused the bike lanes to be removed in less than three months.

Folsom relaunch

However, the City of Boulder has not given up on the idea of adding a protected bike lane to Folsom Street. On November 29, 2024, Boulder Transportation Connections published an article announcing a new project that planned to increase cyclist safety on Folsom St. The first phase of the project in 2024 spent a lot of resources learning about what needs improvement in the community. Interestingly, a large portion of the webpage is dedicated to explaining how this project is different from the previous Folsom Street Living Lab experiment. While this project has not begun development, the city is in the process of deciding on a design.

Iris Avenue

Renovations for Iris Avenue are also planned. The City has already chosen a design for this project, which will add two-way protected bike lanes along the corridor between Broadway and 28th Street. In order to avoid the same pushback as the original Folsom Street renovation project, the project website contains detailed information about how the project will affect travel time for drivers. Unlike the original Folsom Street proposal, the impacts on travel time for drivers appear minor. Although construction has been approved, the start date is unconfirmed.

Baseline Road

The last road being improved by the city is Baseline Road. This project has been split into two phases. The first phase was completed in 2023, and the second phase is planned to be completed in 2026. The first phase allowed the city to narrow the car lanes and add the first iteration of a protected bike lane. Construction for phase 2 is planned to start in the Summer/Fall of 2025.

Beyond concrete: education

However, infrastructure improvements are not the only way that we can improve cycling safety in Boulder. Education is also important in improving cycling safety. Current evidence suggests that classroom education is highly effective at improving cycling knowledge, skills, and confidence. All of these results will lead to fewer cycling accidents. The City of Boulder has already attempted to improve cycling education in school classrooms. Currently, BVSD teaches both the BVSD BLAST and Bikeology programs. These programs teach basic and then advanced cycling skills to 4th through 7th graders. For Adults, CU Boulder offers cycling education programs.
